What to keep an eye on

Beyond dates, qualifications, apply steps, funding, max amounts, and URLs – these fields also break CalClaim when stale.

    Disaster CalFresh windows

    Scanned daily and published automatically – nothing here waits on you. USDA FNS is the agency that approves Disaster CalFresh and the only source that states the application dates, so it drives the decision; FEMA supplies the declaration used to corroborate it and CDSS supplies the phone number. A window goes live on its own once every mechanical check passes, and stops matching once its last apply day passes. Held means the checks did not pass, so the card stayed hidden – the extraction or the source looks wrong, and the failed check is listed on the card below.

    How the difficulty rank and the open/closed status are worked out

    Library is the frozen programs.json row: apply URL, source citation count, deadline count (a * means at least one deadline has no date), and open scan findings from the last library check.

    Open now? is computed on every load, not stored, so it cannot go stale. Dormant means a disaster-gated program like Disaster CalFresh has no county window taking applications – the normal state most of the year, and the offer card stays hidden until CDSS approves one. Window open names the counties and the last day to apply. Deadline soon fires 60 days out; deadline passed means every deadline in the library is behind us, which usually means the date needs rolling to the next cycle rather than the program being gone. Seasonal covers programs like LIHEAP that only open during a county's funding window. Open the cell to pin paused or closed by hand and leave a note saying who told you.

    Score = documents (third-party documents like a doctor's certification or a landlord signature count double; when a program accepts an award letter or pay stubs, that alternative path is scored once) + interview weight (none 0, phone-if-questions 1.5, phone required 3, appointment 3.5, in-person 4.5, home assessment 5) + form minutes ÷ 15 + 0.2 per eligibility rule + 1.5 if another program has to come first. Documents and interviews dominate on purpose – a long rule list decides whether you qualify, not how hard the paperwork is. Easy is 6.5 or less, moderate up to 10, hard above that. Use the tier dropdown on a row to pin a tier when the score reads wrong.

    In the Documents needed column and on the coverage grid, programs that accept either path show Award letter OR pay stubs as one chip / column. Tax credits list a filed tax return explicitly – that is not the same as pay-stub income proof.

    Also qualifies you for is the categorical-eligibility graph: enrolling in the row's program is accepted as proof for those programs, so they should be offered next. Needs first is the reverse – a hard prerequisite.
